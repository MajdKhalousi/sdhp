# Decision 008 — Product Scope

**Status:** Approved
**Last updated:** Phase 0B

## Purpose

Records the approved boundary between what Elaji Health does now, what's coming next, what's a longer-term strategic direction, and what is explicitly excluded.

## Decision

Full scope tables (Core / Near-Term / Strategic / Out of Scope Now): [../Product-Scope.md](../Product-Scope.md).

Summary:
- **Current scope:** a Clinic Operating System for small and medium private clinics, covering daily clinic operations from reception to doctor to invoice to reporting, with basic HR and administration support.
- **Near-term:** real notifications, follow-up reminder delivery, BRANCH_ADMIN clarification, permissions matrix improvement, documented product flows, a QA checklist, a queue display screen, better administrative reports, first-clinic usability improvements.
- **Strategic (not scheduled):** inventory/stock management, pharmacy, offline-first local clinic server, advanced multi-branch management, patient portal, patient mobile access, unified patient medical record across clinics, inter-clinic data sharing with consent, AI assistant, insurance, advanced accounting, enterprise hospital support.
- **Out of scope now:** a full national health platform, ministry integrations, large hospital systems, diagnostic medical AI, a doctor/patient marketplace, a full mobile app, support for every type of medical facility, medical device integrations, a full telemedicine platform, generic ERP outside healthcare.

## Why This Matters

Without an explicit boundary, "strategic" ideas tend to quietly become "near-term" work without anyone deciding that should happen. This decision makes the boundary explicit and approved, so moving something from Strategic to Near-Term (or rejecting something as genuinely Out of Scope) is a deliberate act, not a drift.
